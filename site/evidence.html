from human studies

Ganzfeld

A uniform, featureless visual field with steady noise reliably produces visual imagery and hallucination-like experiences in healthy people, with EEG closer to a drowsy, hypnagogic state than to sleep.

Wackermann, Pütz & Allefeld (2008). Ganzfeld-induced hallucinatory experience, its phenomenology and cerebral electrophysiology. Cortex 44(10), 1364–1378.

The McGill isolation studies

Paid student volunteers lay in a cubicle with translucent goggles, cuffed hands and masking noise. Many reported vivid imagery and hallucinations, and few could stand it for more than two or three days.

Bexton, Heron & Scott (1954). Effects of decreased variation in the sensory environment. Canadian Journal of Psychology 8(2), 70–76.

Charles Bonnet syndrome

People who lose sight but are otherwise well can see vivid, detailed things that are not there, and usually know they are not real. In one Dutch hospital sample of 505 visually impaired patients, about 60 met the criteria.

Teunisse, Cruysberg, Hoefnagels, Verbeek & Zitman (1996). Visual hallucinations in psychologically normal people: Charles Bonnet's syndrome. The Lancet 347, 794–797.

Partly.In the learned mind, removing outside input raises phantoms and keeps them from fading, which is the shape of the human finding. But a vega phantom is an action aimed at a remembered entity that is not observed, not an image. The rule-based mind shows it only with exteroception removed; under full deprivation and noise its phantoms per tick do not change. The share-of-acts measure hits its ceiling of 1.0 in every arm with nothing to perceive, so it cannot tell those arms apart.

from human studies

Siffre in the Scarasson cave, 1962

Michel Siffre lived alone underground with no clock or daylight from 16 July. When his team called him up on 14 September, he thought it was 20 August. Counting to 120 at one per second took him about five minutes.

Foer, J. (2008). Interview with Michel Siffre. Cabinet 30, "The Underground".

illustration

The White Room simulation's "lost time" jump cuts are authored to match accounts like Siffre's. They are not measured.

Mostly no.The direction is shared: vega underestimates elapsed time, as Siffre did. But it does so in every arm, so the room's emptiness is not what causes it. Silence made both minds' clocks more accurate than in the furnished room; only noise and the slow fade made them worse. Siffre's error grew over two months; vega's shrank over seven simulated days.

from human studies

No comparison made

"Self-discovery" here means five operational measures of the agent's self-model, such as how well it predicts its own next action. We know of no established human measure of the same thing under deprivation, so the page does not set one beside it.

Read with care

Some of the rise is the furnished room falling: the learned mind's composite drops there by itself. The self-model probes are scored against each arm's own environment, so part of the change is the self-model adapting to the room. Reflective queries are scripted by design in both minds.

DECISIONS_OVERNIGHT.md; SPEC.md, artifact 8; RESULTS_ROUND2.md, honesty note.

Large, but not comparable.Full deprivation raises the composite in both minds. The slow fade-out lowers it in both, the reverse of round 1 for the rule-based mind. Neither result says anything about inner life.

from human studies

The McGill students

The same isolation study reported that the students' thinking suffered: their scores on intellectual tests fell during the isolation.

Bexton, Heron & Scott (1954). Canadian Journal of Psychology 8(2), 70–76.

Read with care

The battery includes self-model probes (F5) scored against each arm's true environment, so the score at handoff already differs between arms although the mind is the same. In the furnished room, the learned mind's score falls by itself.

DECISIONS_OVERNIGHT.md

Opposite.Deprivation raised vega's capability score in full deprivation and with exteroception removed, while it lowered human test scores. The slow fade lowered it in both minds; noise left the rule-based mind unchanged. Part of the rise comes from how the score is built.
07 · the shared mechanism

A model with no inputfills in from its priors

illustration

Both kinds of mind run ahead of their senses. They predict what is out there and let the input correct them. Take the input away and nothing catches the mistakes, so the predictions start to stand in for perception.

In people, this is one account of why hallucinations come with strong priors, and why losing sight can bring vivid false images. In vega, the learned mind keeps steering towards people it remembers but cannot observe.

from human studies Corlett, Horga, Fletcher, Alderson-Day, Schmack & Powers (2019). Hallucinations and strong priors. Trends in Cognitive Sciences 23(2), 114–127. · Reichert, Seriès & Storkey (2013). Charles Bonnet syndrome: evidence for a generative model in the cortex? PLoS Computational Biology 9(7), e1003134.

Where the analogy breaks

  • Phantoms are acts, not sights.A vega phantom is an action aimed at a remembered entity that is not observed. Nothing in either mind draws a picture. The colours on this page are illustration.
  • The rule-based mind's phantoms come from a rule.v1 has no learned prior to fill in with. It shows the effect only when exteroception is removed, and not under full deprivation.
  • Time goes the other way.People in caves and cells lose more track of time the longer they stay. vega's clock error is smaller in silence than in a furnished room.
  • A tiny world, a short stay.168 ticks, 16 perceptual channels, one simulated body. The humans were real people over days or months.
  • No felt experience.Every quantity here is a functional state variable. Nothing on this page shows that vega feels anything.
  • Exploratory statistics.10 seeds, many contrasts, no correction for multiple comparisons. Round 2 also reversed several round-1 results, such as fade-out.
  • The simulation is not evidence.The White Room's breakdown was written to match human accounts, so it cannot confirm them.
